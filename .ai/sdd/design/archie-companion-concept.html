Here's what's circulating: several model builders announced cheaper inference this week, and three separate outlets used the same 19th-century economist to frame it. That's Fact a real, observable pattern in this week's coverage — not something I'm inferring.

Fact William Stanley Jevons argued in The Coal Question (1865) that more efficient steam engines could increase total coal use, by making it cheaper to run an engine at all — efficiency expanding demand, not shrinking it.

Conceptual analogy Writers are applying that same shape to AI: if inference gets cheaper, total compute demand might rise rather than fall. This is a structural analogy between two different systems 160 years apart — I'm not claiming Jevons said anything about data centers, and neither should the story that leans on him.

How to read the tags: Fact verifiable record  ·  Interpretation a defensible reading of the record  ·  Hypothesis untested, stated as a guess  ·  Analogy a structural parallel, not a causal claim

Where this is genuinely contested

  • Interpretation Some energy economists read the Jevons parallel as basically sound for AI compute — cheaper tokens, more total tokens run.
  • Interpretation Others argue the analogy overreaches: Jevons was describing a single fungible fuel market, not a landscape of differently-priced model tiers with hard efficiency ceilings from power and chip supply. Both readings appear in this week's coverage; I'm not picking a winner for you.
