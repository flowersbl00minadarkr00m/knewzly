Non-binding design spike · proposed

Make the history of AI feel connected, not crowded.

A document-first explanation of the proposed atlas interaction: one shared time axis, parallel regional lanes, a persistent philosophy lane, typed relationships, and a drawer that keeps context visible.

Source state: project plan approved. F01 requirements, design, tasks, and implementation are not approved or present. All interactive data in this explainer is simulated.

The user outcome

Knewzly should help a curious learner explain where an innovation came from, who or what shaped it, and what carried forward across regions and time.

The first learning loop Orient on time → compare lanes → trace one typed relationship → inspect context and evidence.
01 / ORIENTFind the momentUse years and lane labels to build a mental map.
02 / COMPARENotice a bridgeSee parallel developments without a single-center story.
03 / TRACERead the edgeUnderstand whether it influenced, enabled, or framed a later step.
04 / INSPECTCheck the claimOpen the drawer for narrative, uncertainty, and sources.

Decisions at a glance

Entry point

History atlas first. Today is attached and bounded, not the home feed.

Default unit

Anchor events keep the canvas readable; medium-depth learning opens on demand.

Relationship language

Typed edges make influence, enablement, iteration, and conceptual lenses distinct.

Trust posture

Source, date, claim type, confidence, and contested status travel with visible claims.

Responsive posture

Wide canvas on desktop; stacked lanes, focused time strip, and full-height drawer on mobile.

Motion posture

Selection can be understood without animation; reduced motion removes transitions.

Architecture and trust boundaries

The spike keeps the product surface understandable without choosing a framework, feed provider, or hosting model. The production boundary remains a later design decision.

Verification plan

The future binding design should be verified with exact checks for selection, focus return, keyboard traversal, mobile drawer behavior, relationship labels, reduced motion, contrast, zoom, and provenance state rendering.

  • Functional: select event, focus relationship, open/close drawer, trace from Today.
  • Accessibility: semantic landmarks, accessible names, visible focus, no color-only meaning, 200% zoom, reduced-motion equivalent.
  • Responsive: wide atlas, narrow stacked lanes, full-height drawer, no horizontal page overflow.
  • Trust: every visible demo claim marked simulated; no source or date fabricated as verified.

This explainer is a planning artifact. Its simulated interactions are not implementation evidence and do not advance the SDD gate.
